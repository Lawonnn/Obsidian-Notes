## Software Engineering # 2
## Waterfall Model
It was a ==strictly linear== approach where you design the entire system, code the entire system, test it, and finally deploy it. If requirements changed halfway through, the project often failed or suffered massive delays
![[Pasted image 20260915151846.png]]

## Agile Manifesto
In 2001, a group of developers frustrated with this rigidity created the Agile Manifesto. It ==prioritizes adaptability, continuous delivery, and iterative improvement==. It is governed by 4 core values:
- **Individuals and interactions** over process and tools
- **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
- **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
- **Responding to change** over following a plan

## Scrum
It is a ==specific way to execute Agile== by breaking large, complex project into small, manageable development cycles called **Sprits** (usually 2 to 4 weeks long)
### Cycle
![[Pasted image 20260915153046.png]]
- **Sprint**: 1 to 4 weeks, is the work period
- **Sprint Planning**: the **Sprint Backlog** and **Sprint Goal** are out together
- **Daily Scrum**: a short, 15min daily meeting to check progress and plan work for the next 24hrs
- **Sprint Review**: the increment is shown in action, the **Product Backlog** is adjusted
- **Sprint Retrospective**: the team look at itself and defines a specific improvement
### Artifacts
- **Product Backlog**: a list of everything the product might need
- **Sprint Backlog**: what the team committed to doing this sprint
- **Increment**: what was actually completed
### Roles
- **Product Owner**
- **Scrum Master**
- **Developers**
### Product Owner
Manage the ==product==: what and in what order

### Project Manager
Manage the ==project==: schedule, budget, risk

## Kanban
The term "kanban" is a japanese term that means *visual sign*. It's a method designed to visualize work, limit multitasking, and optimize process efficiency. ![[Pasted image 20260915155550.jpg]]
### Core Structure
- **Kanban board**: A visual framework divided into vertical columns representing sequential stages of execution (**Backlog**, **To Do**, **In Progress**, **Done**)
- **Kanban Cards**: Visual elements representing individual work items or tickets
- **Work in Progress (WIP) limits**: Hard constraints applied to specific columns (eg, maximum 3 tasks in **In Progress** ).

## When use Kanban or Scrum?
- Use **Scrum** when work can be planned in iterative goals and the team can commit to a fixed scope for 1-4 weeks
- Use **Kanban** when work comes in unpredictably and you need to respond quickly
