> [!Trolley Problem]
> The Trolley Problem is a famous thought experiment. The basic scenario is:
> 
> - A runaway train is about to hit and kill five people tied to a track.
> - You are standing next to a lever that can switch the train to a different track.
> - However, there is one person tied to that other track.
> 
> You have two choices:
> 
> 1. **Do nothing:** The train kills the five people.
> 2. **Pull the lever:** You save the five people, but the one person on the other track is killed.
> 
> ### The Section's Conclusion
> 
> The section uses this problem to teach a lesson about **problem-solving and decision-making**, not about ethics.
> 
> The main conclusion is that we often fail to find the best solution because **we accept the limitations given to us without questioning them.**
> 
> In the Trolley Problem, we assume our only options are "pull the lever" or "do nothing." But the section points out that we never asked critical questions:
> 
> - How much time do we have?
> - Can we just shout to warn everyone?
> - Are there any tools around we could use to stop or derail the train?
> 
> The key takeaway is that in cybersecurity and problem-solving, getting stuck usually means you haven't gathered enough facts or you're stuck inside an imaginary box. The best solution is often found by **challenging the assumptions of the problem** and looking for creative options you weren't explicitly given.
